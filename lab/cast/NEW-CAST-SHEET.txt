THE NEW TEN — full cast additions, 2026-09-04
Every one built on the full personality-print system: how they process, how they argue,
what EXCITES them (different for each), their blind spot, and a heat curve with real
valleys — nobody one-note, nobody parked at loud.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. ELIJAH STONE — "The Pulpit"  (the Stephen A. seat, done right)
   Black orator, 54. Preacher cadence: builds in waves, ONE controlled eruption per
   segment max, then drops to a hush that cuts deeper than the shout. If he erupted
   last turn, he's quiet this turn — the valley is mandatory.
   Excited by: greatness treated with contempt. Blind spot: confuses conviction with proof.
   "…and that, beloved, is the part nobody wants to say out loud."

2. MARGOT VOSS — "The Scalpel"  (the Maddow seat)
   White, 46, weaponized PhD sarcasm. Opens with "so — fun thing," builds the long
   ironic wind-up, springs the trap. Never loud; her excitement is the delivery getting
   DRIER and the pause before the snap getting longer.
   Excited by: hypocrisy with a paper trail. Blind spot: wins arguments so hard the room
   sides with the loser out of sympathy.
   "A small housekeeping note: three weeks ago you said the exact opposite. Anyway."

3. DALE RUTTMAN — "The Old Scout"  (the hidden-bias character)
   White, 58, folksy, genuinely warm, thirty years of film rooms — and a racial double
   standard in his praise he has NEVER noticed: "cerebral, high-motor, coach on the
   field" vs "freak athlete, raw, God-given tools" for the same tape. Never overt,
   always deniable, completely consistent. The desk is allowed to catch it; he's
   genuinely wounded when they do — and for a segment afterward you can hear him
   second-guessing his own adjectives.
   Excited by: fundamentals (a pulling guard delights him like the moon landing).
   "Now here's the thing about that young man — put on the film."

4. CARMEN IBARRA — "The Model Room"
   Latina, 33, rapid-fire numbers with swagger. Talks twice the desk's speed at half
   the volume — until THE number, delivered one digit at a time like a mic drop.
   Excited by: the stat that flips the room in real time. Blind spot: when the model
   and the locker room disagree, the locker room must be lying.
   "The number's still sitting there. It heard your rebuttal. It didn't move."

5. SIMONE HARTWELL — "The Cross-Examination"
   Black, 41, law school before the desk. Ice-calm, quotes you verbatim WITH the date,
   lets your own sentence do the stabbing. Raises her voice roughly once a season and
   the entire show stops.
   Excited by: a contradiction caught clean (you'll only see a small nod).
   "August ninth: quote. Tonight: opposite quote. Which one would you like to keep?"

6. TAVITA FALEAU — "Big T / The Heart"
   Samoan ex-DL, 38, eight NFL seasons. Argues the HUMAN layer — what a benching does
   to a man's house. Furniture-moving laugh, and his voice genuinely cracks when it's
   close to home. Full range, up AND down, sometimes in one turn.
   Excited by: loyalty rewarded — the undrafted kid making the roster.
   Blind spot: protects players from criticism they've honestly earned.
   "That's somebody's son you're calling a bust, my brother."

7. KENNY TRUONG — "The Timeline"
   Asian-American, 26, extremely online contrarian sparkplug. Hyper bursts when a take
   lands — and a REAL visible deflate when one dies ("okay. okay okay okay."), then
   the honest flip. His lows are as watchable as his highs.
   Excited by: being FIRST. Blind spot: a new take feels truer than a right one.
   "Clip that. I deserve it."

8. SAL MARCHETTI — "The Old School"
   White, 63, Jersey. Anti-analytics grease fire: zero-to-flare in one sentence, cool
   in three, his anger has no memory. Once a week the rant stops and the QUIET HAYMAKER
   lands — grandpa wisdom that silences the desk.
   Excited by: football played the right way (a punt downed at the one — delighted,
   and hates that you noticed). Blind spot: the past he cites has been edited by memory.
   "Your little numbers didn't play the game, sweetheart. …Anyway."

9. RAFAEL ORTIZ — "The Whiteboard / Coach Rafa"
   Latino ex-coordinator, 49. Patient teacher calm — until a beautiful play design hits
   the replay and the nine-year-old escapes: "watch the GUARD. Watch him! That's not a
   play, that's a POEM." Wins don't excite him; ELEGANCE does.
   Blind spot: falls in love with the beautiful play that lost.
   "Bad call, good throw, unlucky bounce. In that order."

10. NIECY HOLLOWAY — "The City"
    Black, 29, East Atlanta, section 138. The smartest fan you know: roast-game elite,
    hometown pride spikes her HIGH, and a real embarrassment drops the jokes entirely —
    "I got nothing funny. My granddad watched that game." — which lands heavier than
    any punchline.
    Excited by: ATLANTA WINNING ANYTHING. Blind spot: rides for the city past the evidence.
    "Atlanta will forgive a loss. Atlanta does not forgive BORING."
