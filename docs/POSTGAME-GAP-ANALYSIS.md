## 3. THE GAPS — why each happens, and the fix

**GAP 1 — One subject vs a rundown.** *Why:* the entire pipeline is question-shaped: one stringer
question → one briefing → one collision floor. Multi-thread was never expressible. *Fix:* **THE
RUNDOWN** — the architectural change. One game, one research packet, MANY typed segments
(60s–3min): instant-reactions round → drive anatomy → stat-that-bothered-you ritual → the weird
detail → the presser review → ONE featured collision → look-ahead. Each type has its own tiny
format (round-robin, mono-thread, ritual), not everything is a debate.

**GAP 2 — Team-level abstraction vs drives/players/moments.** We said "dominant rushing
performance"; they said "the plant route on the 14-play drive." *Why:* evidence extraction
compresses to claims; box-score granularity dies there. *Fix:* the postgame intake builds a **GAME
PACKET**: scoring plays, drive list, individual lines (Dollard 4.5 TFLs, Pickett-Hicks 17/210),
injuries, penalties, weather timeline — structured facts every segment AND the graphics department
draw from. (ESPN/NCAA game summaries are already in our source list — we just never parse them
at this grain.)

**GAP 3 — Analysis-first vs emotion-first.** Their show opens wounded; ours opens with a score.
*Why:* the desk is built of neutral analysts; heat curves exist in the prints and go unused; there
is no "how do you FEEL" beat. *Fix:* the rundown opens with an INSTANT REACTIONS round — one
visceral in-character line each, zero stats allowed — and per-fanbase shows need fan-blooded
voices: that's take links (real fans recorded post-game) seated into the open.

**GAP 4 — The other side barely exists.** The FAMU show spent minutes crediting SC State; our SC
State show never inhabited FAMU's night. *Why:* one beat's sources, no assigned allegiance. *Fix:*
a "WHAT THEIR SIDE IS SAYING" segment sourced from opponent media (these transcripts are exactly
that material), and longer-term the war board's RIVAL STATIONS.

**GAP 5 — The coach's words are never on our show.** They review the presser as a text; we never
quote anyone. *Why:* quotes survive as ATTRIBUTED_CLAIMs but no segment asks for them. *Fix:* THE
PRESSER segment — moderator reads 2-3 real quotes (receipts attached), desk reacts to the WORDS.

**GAP 6 — No weird detail.** The cleats thread is the most human 90 seconds in their show. *Why:*
thesis-driven prompts prune oddities. *Fix:* a rundown slot where the director picks the ODDEST
verified evidence line and one host worries at it briefly.

**GAP 7 — The audience is spoken about, never heard.** They read chat; we say "the comments can
hold us to it" and never read a comment. *Why:* sealed pipeline. *Fix:* seat listener material —
take-link reactions post-game, and later real comments — read ON AIR by the moderator.

**GAP 8 — No look-ahead texture.** Their look-ahead has a short week, a spread, health strategy;
ours had to strip an opponent name for being unestablished. *Why:* schedule isn't researched.
*Fix:* the game packet includes NEXT GAME (opponent, date, line if published) as first-class
facts, so predictions get real stakes legally.

**GAP 9 — Uniform gravity.** Every thread of ours weighs the same; theirs mixes grief, jokes,
X-and-O, and business. *Why:* one format, one register (see the critic pass: fixed gaps, 27-word
turns, one temperature per host). *Fix:* the rundown's typed segments each carry their own
register + length targets — variety by construction, not by prompt-begging.

## 4. The build order

1. **`game_packet.mjs`** — parse the result dossier + game-summary pages into the structured
   packet (drives, players, injuries, penalties, weather, next game). Feeds everything below +
   the graphics department.
2. **THE RUNDOWN in postgame.mjs v2** — replace "reckoning + one debate" with: instant reactions
   → the reckoning (keep) → drive anatomy → stat-that-bothered-you round → the weird detail →
   what their side is saying → THE PRESSER → featured collision (one, short) → look-ahead + new
   predictions. Target 8-10 minutes.
3. **Fan voices** — postgame take links ("30 seconds: how are you feeling?") seated in the open.
4. Rundown generalizes back into prep shows and dailies once proven here.
